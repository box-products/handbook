## What Shaping looks like at SALT
I (David Link) am putting my thoughts down here for how I envision what Shaping will look like for us. We have discussed that we don’t intend to fully adopt the process laid out in Shape Up, but we also acknowledge that there are valuable concepts from which we can draw in order conceive a system that will work for us.


## Why do we want to do this?
During the first few years of SALT, we have operated in a start-up, bootstrappy, fly-by-the-seat-of-our-pants mode. Our project goals have been relatively clear, but our methods for achieving those goals have been unplanned, disjointed, and often meandering.

## What do we want to get out of this?
The short answer — better planning. We need to develop a methodical system that will allow us to:

* Establish the truth of the problem, what’s in play, and what’s off limits
* Judge the value of solving this problem, who does it help?
* Determine where the project fits within our current priorities
* Decide how much development time is worth expending on this project
* Produce a high level solution to the problem
* Identify any potential rabbit holes or pitfalls that may be encountered during development

In short, after shaping, we should have a clear understanding of a project’s:

  1. Problem
  2. Value
  3. Priority
  4. Appetite
  5. Solution (minimally detailed)
  6. Diversions

## Who is going to do this?
At this stage, as our development team consists of only 3 people who happen to be what amounts to the Product Lead, the Backend Lead, and the UX Lead. It’s my perception that all 3 of us should be involved in the shaping process for every project moving forward. I believe this to be important because:

### Collaboration
Each of us possess a different perspective, skillset, and creative bent. Our various points of view will be critical in determining the best path forward. Even when a particular project may be beyond someone’s typical domain, that person’s outside viewpoint can often generate a reflection or refraction leading to unimagined ideas, spur deeper exploration, or act as a guide rail to keep the solution focused.

### Insight
Beyond the value of collaborative effort, I believe it is also important that the 3 of us all stay on the same page. This ensures each of us moves forward in our decision-making with a complete understanding of how it may affect other moving pieces.

To digress: Because I am least involved in the goings-on of SALT’s backend and infrastructure, many changes have often been made without my knowledge. This, in and of itself, is not necessarily a problem. However, the collateral effect is I am then left without an up-to-date understanding of how the inner workings of SALT function. Sometimes this isn’t an issue. But it could potentially mean that something I’m building no longer works, or perhaps I may have done something differently had I been aware, etc.

This example could be applied to any of us. The point I’m trying to make is that each of us are able to make better decisions when we’re all fully aware of the other projects that are underway.

### Support
This is a bit of an extension of **Insight**. And, actually, I am referring to two different types of support here.

	1. Developer support — When I know how Blake’s project will develop and when he knows how mine will, we can provide to support to one another when needed. We’ll know at what point in the other’s project that we may be asked for guidance or collaboration. It’ll also mean that we can work on our own projects with a full picture of the future in mind, as we all will have a better understanding of what lies ahead for the app.
	2. Customer Support — Much like the above, when we all have a more complete understanding about how all aspects of the app function, and of the updates (in-progress or planned), we can provide more meaningful support to our customers, and will be able to more precisely and succinctly describe the issue so it can be addressed accordingly.


## How are we going to do this?
I imagine this process will be somewhat fluid, so while the list below will naturally happen chronologically, it’s not necessarily something that needs to happen in distinct and separate steps.

	1. Describe
	2. Discuss
	3. Explore
	4. Plan
	5. Delegate

**Describe** — The way I envision this working is that the initial idea is written up in broad detail. This could be in the form of a GitHub issue, or just a text document. The idea is explained briefly and abstractly. I’m not sure if it’s beneficial or detrimental for the author to add any of their own thoughts regarding the solution. It may be best to withhold those until all 3 of us have reviewed the initial problem/idea.

**Discuss** — The 3 of us get on a call to discuss. We could do this as needed, or we could bundle ideas into a slate and just spend a day hashing them all out. It’s at this point we can determine the Value, the Priority, and the Appetite for this work. I think at this point, if we decide the work should move forward, it should be made into a GitHub issue if it hasn’t already. We can use the template that Jonathan has provided for now, adapting it as we see fit. I believe it will be natural for some thoughts about the Solution to arise during this conversation, but unless we intend to move immediately into the Exploration process, it may be best to keep those solutions quite abstract.

**Explore** — This phase can either occur immediately following the Discussion, or it can be saved for another day if additional time commitment is not possible or not desired. During this phase, I imagine we use visual tools to begin laying out the solution. Simple flow carts, notes, and fat marker sketches can be used to illustrate the idea generically.

**Plan** — At the end of the Exploration process, we should know more precisely what we want to fix. From there we can plan out the things that will be off-limits, and a list of potential rabbit holes and pitfalls. This can be expressed as a list of front-end and backend considerations/concerns and be attached to the relevant GitHub Issue.

**Delegate** — When we feel we’ve explored the problem and solution to a substantial degree, we can plan out who will be working on what parts and when. This should be a straightforward and mostly obvious process at this stage, since our dev team consists of only one front-end and one backend developer (with Jonathan doing both on an ad-hoc basis). But we will need to determine timetables for the start and completion of this work.
